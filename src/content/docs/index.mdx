## What HotLoop is

An automation platform for the plant floor, written from scratch in Go. It polls PLCs and instruments over the protocols they already speak, keeps every reading with its quality, raises ISA-18.2 alarms, runs automations, and lets an agent read the whole plant over MCP. Every write to a machine goes through one gate, and every tag starts read-only, because the worst possible way to meet new software is watching it move a press you never told it to touch.

It ships as four products out of one codebase: IoT, Edge, Gateway and Edge Relay. HotLoop Flow is its own product: Node-RED's idea on a Go runtime, under Apache-2.0.

  <Card title="HotLoop Gateway 4.17.0" icon="setting">
    The whole site in one box: eight native protocols (read-only UniFi is the newest), a historian, alarms, recipes, scripts, a logbook, fleet, multi-site, reports and MCP.   <Card title="HotLoop Edge Relay 4.17.0" icon="rocket">
    Headless and dumb on purpose. It polls with the Gateway's drivers, forwards over Sparkplug B, and buffers to disk when the uplink dies. No database, no UI, no write path. [Install it](/edge-relay/install/) with Helm or a Quadlet unit.
  </Card>
  <Card title="HotLoop Flow 2.0.5" icon="random">
    Your Node-RED `flows.json`, loaded unchanged, in a 25.1 MB image instead of 717 MB. Every queue has a ceiling, and it refuses to start without a login unless you opt out on purpose. [Install it](/flow/install/), or [bring your flows over](/flow/migrating-from-node-red/).
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

HotLoop IoT and HotLoop Edge aren't out. There's no image to pull, so there's no install page, because a command for an image that doesn't exist is worse than no command at all. Most of what they'll carry already runs in the Gateway 4.17.0, helpers, scripts, recipes, blueprints and the logbook included, and IoT waits on MQTT discovery, which isn't built. ## How to read these docs

"Verified" means it ran against the real thing: a real OPC UA server, a real broker, Ignition itself. Anything tested against a simulator or a stand-in says exactly that. S7comm and EtherNet/IP have never talked to a physical PLC, and [Protocols](/gateway/protocols/) tells you so before it tells you how to use them.

Gateway and Edge Relay pages describe 4.17.0, and Flow pages describe 2.0.5, unless the top of the page says otherwise. Planning a rollout around something? Check that first, not after the upgrade turns out not to have it.

Status for everything, product by product, lives in the [release notes](https://hotloop.io/releases/) and the [integrations table](https://hotloop.io/integrations/).
